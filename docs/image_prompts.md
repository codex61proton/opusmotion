# THE MOON FILES — image-generation prompts for optional photoreal plates

The finished MP4 is complete without these. Every shot was made in code: vector diagrams, a GLSL globe and Moon, and stylised period illustration. Four things code cannot do convincingly are photorealistic **people, period architecture, weathered materials, and night-time practical lighting**. The prompts below are for optional plates that would replace or sit behind those illustrations in a future cut.

## Rules for every plate

- **Format:** 9:16 vertical, generate at 2160×3840 (minimum 1080×1920). Plates sit *under* animated overlays.
- **Caption-safe area:** the top 30% of the frame (y 0–580 at 1080×1920) must be low-detail, dark negative space. Platform UI covers the bottom 18%, so keep key subjects between 35% and 80% of frame height.
- **No text of any kind:** no signage, no lettering on vehicles, no labels, no watermarks. All text is added in the edit for accuracy.
- **Period:** 1959–1965. No modern vehicles, LEDs, plastics, smartphones, safety vests, or contemporary clothing.
- **Look:** photographic, 35 mm, natural film grain, slightly lifted blacks, restrained saturation. Never stylised, illustrated, HDR or "cinematic orange-teal" overprocessing.
- **Factual guardrails:** do not depict identifiable real people. Do not invent insignia. Do not show weapons, guards or violence (the sources mention none). The Lunik stage must follow the CIA layout drawing: a cone nose over a cylindrical body about 17.5 ft long and 8.4 ft wide, with three small rectangular inspection windows in the nose cone.
- **Negative prompt (all plates):** `text, letters, signage, logo, watermark, modern car, LED, smartphone, high-visibility vest, plastic, CGI look, illustration, cartoon, oversaturated, HDR, lens flare spam, extra limbs, distorted hands, futuristic`

## P1 — The 150-foot dish at Stanford, night (replaces wireframe dish, 35.9–43.2 s and 43.5–52.2 s)

> Photograph, 1965, night. A 150-foot steerable parabolic radio dish on an open-truss alidade stands in dry golden foothill grassland west of Palo Alto, California. The dish is tilted about 40 degrees toward the upper left of frame, where a small, bright, nearly full Moon hangs in a deep blue-black sky. The dish surface is mesh over a radial steel truss, with a four-legged feed support converging at the focus. The scene is lit only by moonlight plus one sodium work lamp at the base, casting a warm pool on the concrete pedestal. Faint stars. A single small human figure stands at the base for scale. Low camera, 24 mm, looking up; the dish fills the middle 50% of the frame vertically; the top 30% is empty sky. Kodak Tri-X pushed one stop, then subtly hand-tinted cool blue; fine grain; no text.

**Usage:** composite under the Faint shot's dish wireframe, then cross-dissolve wireframe to plate over 0.6 s.

In the background, out-of-focus shapes suggest other industrial exhibits (turbines, machine tools). Kodak Ektachrome 1959 colour, warm tungsten, fine grain.

## Hand-off notes

- Generate each plate at least twice with different seeds. Pick the variant whose hardware matches the CIA Figure 1 silhouette; reject anything with fins, nozzles, or a pointed body longer than about 2:1.
- Grade plates to the film's palette before compositing: blacks to `#05070B`, highlights capped at 92%, with the same grain amount as the GLSL pass (`grainAmt` 0.06–0.085). Oversized plates can carry a slow 6% push-in.
- Label any plate used in the film as a reconstruction in the on-screen footer (e.g. "ILLUSTRATIVE RECONSTRUCTION").
