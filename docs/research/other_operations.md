# Researched but not used in this film: candidates for future episodes

These are condensed from three background dossiers (completed after the film was locked). Confidence notes are carried over. Verify against the primary sources before scripting.

## GUNMAN: Soviet bugs in US embassy typewriters (1976–84). Evidence: strong.
- **Primary sources.** Maneki, *Learning from the Enemy: The GUNMAN Project* (NSA, 2012); Johnson, *American Cryptology during the Cold War*, Bk IV, pp. 401–07.
- **The swap.** In 1984 NSA swapped out all of the Moscow embassy's sensitive equipment under Reagan's approval: about 10 tons in, about 11 out, over roughly 10 days. The Soviets cut the elevator after day one, so the rest moved by hand. Typewriters were an afterthought.
- **The discovery.** On 23–24 Jul 1984, Michael Arneson X-rayed a Selectric and saw an "extra coil on the power switch". The bug sat in a hollow aluminium comb-support bar. Six magnetometers read magnet-tipped latch interposers. It buffered 8 four-bit characters and sent bursts at 30/60/90 MHz near TV carriers, and could be switched off remotely.
- **Scale.** 16 bugged machines (Moscow 13, Leningrad 3), five generations. It was blind to space, shift and the hyphen at the home position, so output was lower-case.
- **Avoid.** "Encrypted frequency-hopping", "30 typewriters", and power-cord transmission are not in NSA's account. Soviet-customs installation is NSA's inference, not proof.

## ENGULF (1956) and STOCKADE (c.1960–64). Evidence: weak (almost all from Wright's *Spycatcher*).
- **ENGULF.** An acoustic attack on the Egyptian embassy's Hagelin through a modified phone ("Special Facilities") while the clerk reset the wheels each morning. Do not name the model (not CX-52). Do not claim it ended Suez.
- **STOCKADE.** A conducted/RF "echo" of high-grade traffic picked up through a plasterboard partition at the French embassy, via a footway-box tap and a Hyde Park Hotel room. The French countered with shielding by late 1964 or early 1965.
- Aldrich, Andrew and Steed add little independent corroboration. Present these as Wright's account.

## PALLADIUM: CIA ghost radar targets (1962–63+). Evidence: Poteat memoirs (*Studies* 42:1, 1998; *The Bent*, 1999).
- **Method.** A received radar pulse was replayed through a variable delay line to create false targets with chosen range, speed and cross-section. Soviet/Cuban reactions were monitored via an NSA COMINT link.
- **Cuba test.** A submarine released calibrated metal spheres on balloons to measure SA-2 sensitivity. The ghost "fighter" was switched off as a Cuban pilot began his firing pass.
- **Dating.** The name dates from Wheelon as DDS&T (Aug 1963 onward), but Poteat places the Cuba run "during the missile crisis". Hedge the date.

## COLDFEET (1962), GENETRIX (1956), HEXAGON RV-3 recovery (1971–72). Evidence: high (Leary; FRUS; Waltrop/NRO).
- **COLDFEET.** Two officers parachuted onto abandoned Soviet drift station NP-8 and were extracted by a Fulton Skyhook B-17 on 2 Jun 1962 (Leary, *Studies* 38:5). Myths to avoid: the balloon lifts only the line; 17 g was the 1943 system, not Skyhook.
- **GENETRIX.** 516 balloons launched and 44 gondolas recovered (~8.5%) over 27 days. Mid-air C-119 catches prefigured satellite film-bucket recovery. The Luna 3 "American balloon film" story rests on one Soviet designer's recollection, so attribute it.
- **HEXAGON.** The RV-3 parachute failed on 10 Jul 1971, and the bucket sank to about 16,400 ft. Trieste II grabbed it on the third dive (25–26 Apr 1972), but the film disintegrated into "reddish brown dust" on ascent. It was a failure for intelligence but proved deep recovery (Waltrop, *Quest* 19:3).
